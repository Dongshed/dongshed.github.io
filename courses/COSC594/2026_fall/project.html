Project Guideline (PDF)

Project Description

Each student completes an individual study of an optimization problem for machine learning, supported by theory, experiments, or both. Choose a problem connected to the course and your research interests, and confirm the scope with the instructor before submitting the proposal. A paper summary or literature review is not a project.

The final outcome is a conference-format research manuscript. Empirical projects also require a reproducible software artifact; theoretical projects do not require code or a software artifact. COSC 594 projects may develop a meaningful, rigorous extension of existing work. COSC 650 projects are expected to make a stronger original theoretical or empirical contribution.

Milestones and Grading

The project is worth 45% of the course grade. The weights below are contributions to the total course grade.

Submit each milestone on Canvas by 11:59 pm Eastern Time on its due date. Check Canvas for your assigned presentation date; slides are due on that date. Late report submissions are not accepted.

Submission Guidelines

  • Proposal: Two pages, excluding references, are recommended. Describe the problem, research question, related work, baseline, and validation plan.

  • Technical checkpoint: Add a one-page progress summary to your proposal. Include initial results and the remaining work plan; empirical projects also include runnable baseline code.

  • Presentation: A 10-minute talk followed by 2 minutes of Q&A. Approximately 10 slides, excluding transition slides, are recommended.

  • Final report: Six pages, excluding references and appendices, are recommended. Use the NeurIPS 2025 LaTeX template with the preprint option, and include your name and affiliation.

  • AI use: Include an AI Use Statement at each stage, documenting assistance and independent verification, or state that no AI tools were used. Follow the full disclosure requirements in the project guideline.
